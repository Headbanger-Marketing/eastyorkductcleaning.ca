Using plans or a hand sketch to show where ducts and vents run

A plan or a simple sketch of each floor of your East York home can show a provider who has never seen it where the furnace or air handler sits, which rooms each register serves and how the dryer vent leaves the house. Some of it may only be a guess, and concealed sections may not show at all, so mark a guess as a guess and leave those sections marked unknown. Then add the items below, so the request can be quoted against the layout you drew.

  1. Return grilles

    Openings that pull room air back into the system.

  2. Return ducts

    The runs leading from each grille to the furnace.

  3. Furnace blower and coil

    Blower assembly, coil and the cabinet around them.

  4. Supply trunk

    The main duct leaving the furnace.

  5. Branch runs

    Smaller ducts feeding each room.

  6. Supply registers

    The floor, wall or ceiling vents in each room.

Separate runs to ask about:Dryer ventHRV core and screensBathroom fan ductsRange hood exhaust

  • Registers and return grilles

    Mark each supply register and return grille by room on the sketch, and note any room you are unsure about.

  • Known and guessed marks

    Label each line known, guessed or unknown, including the dryer vent route and the outside hoods you can see from outside.

Questions About Your East York Request

Who is East York Duct Cleaning, and does it clean ducts itself?

East York Duct Cleaning has no office, staff or vehicles and does none of the work itself, so it is an enquiry website, not a contractor. Our enquiry team reviews each request and may forward it to a provider. Whoever responds must identify themselves, confirm they cover your address and agree scope and pricing with you in writing.

Who receives my East York request, and can they book anything on my behalf?

You may decline any provider who responds, and each must agree the scope, pricing and timing with you before work is arranged. Sending gives our enquiry team your description, drawing and photos to review, without booking a visit, setting a price or confirming that a provider covers your address.

Do I need a floor plan, or is a hand sketch enough?

Neither is required: a written description of the rooms, registers and what you noticed is enough to send. If you want to show the layout, a rough sketch of each floor works well. Draw rooms as boxes, mark the furnace or air handler, each register and return grille, and the dryer vent route, then label each mark known or guessed. Add any plan you already hold, but do not hunt for one.

How do I draw parts of my East York ducts that I cannot see?

Leave them marked unknown. Do not open walls, ceilings or panels to find out, and do not draw a route without labelling it. A provider can later confirm or correct the unknown sections with access you approve, and the written scope should say which sections remain unconfirmed.

Can one East York request cover the dryer vent and the other ducts?

Yes, a single description can mention both, as long as each appears as its own line on the sketch. The vent and the furnace ducts do not share a run, so ask the provider to list the two separately in the written scope, along with the outside hoods, and say which items are part of the agreed work. Ask whether the vent route on your sketch matches what the provider finds.

What if my sketch turns out to be wrong?

That is expected, which is why each line is labelled known or guessed. The provider compares the drawing with what is visible and notes differences in the written record. A wrong guess changes the scope discussion, not your standing, so correct it and ask the provider to explain any change before approving it. Keep your original sketch so the changes can be followed.

Who can approve work if I rent or share the building?

Whoever holds authority over the space. Owners decide for their own home; tenants may need the landlord or property manager, and condominium owners may need the corporation. Name that person in your request and ask the provider to confirm who signs the written scope. Questions about lease terms belong with the landlord or a lawyer.

What should I photograph to go with my sketch?

Photograph the supply registers and return grilles, the equipment label, the dryer connector, outside hoods and any visible joints or access panels, naming the room in each file. Photos support the drawing but do not replace it. Skip anything you would need to open, move or disconnect to reach, and say so in the request. A clear photo of each label, taken straight on, is more useful than a wide shot of the room.
